
====================================================================================================
LONG-TERM FAIRNESS IN RIDE-HAILING  --  GAP IMPLEMENTATION RESULTS
====================================================================================================
Base paper : Kang, Chan, Shao, Salim, Leckie, ECML PKDD 2024 (arXiv:2407.17839)
Dataset    : NYC yellow taxi, March 2016 (12,210,952 -> 10,300,738 trips)
Setup      : 87 graph nodes, 200 simulated drivers (76 full-time / 124 part-time),
             7-day horizon 25-31 Mar, 5-minute decision epochs, offered load 0.60

====================================================================================================
STEP 1  WHY THE PAPER'S UTILITY IS WRONG  (Gap 1a, measured)
====================================================================================================
The paper flattens travel time to a period mean (Sec. 5.1), making a trip
worth the same at 04:00 and 18:00. Measured on its own dataset:
  citywide speed        28.5 km/h at 04:00  vs  12.8 km/h at 12:00 = 2.22x
  same OD pair, peak/off-peak travel time   median 2.11x, max 4.35x
  utility sign flips on 10.2% of (driver, request, time) triples
     -> the paper calls 1 request in 10 profitable when it is not

====================================================================================================
STEP 2  WHY THE PAPER'S FAIRNESS METRIC IS BLIND  (Gap 1b, measured)
====================================================================================================
Paper Eq. 2 is Var(weekly TOTAL utility), which ignores hours worked.
On this fleet, equalising totals (what Eq. 2 drives towards) gives:
  Var(total earnings) = 0.0        -> 'perfectly fair'
  Var(hourly rate)    = 7,859      -> rates span 47.9 to 375.7 = 7.8x gap
  by group: full-time 57.3/h  vs  part-time 205.6/h = 3.59x

ROBUSTNESS
====================================================================================================
1) Money-denominated utility (fare = $3.95/km, measured):
     full-time  $  6.62/h  ->  $  8.20/h
     part-time  $ 12.35/h  ->  $  7.93/h
     ratio        1.865    ->    0.968
     The gap survives the change of units, so it is a property of
     the allocation, not of distance-denominated utility.

2) Seed sensitivity (3 independent seeds: fleet, requests and RL exploration all re-drawn):
            utility  rate_var   between     ratio  ratio_sd   utiladj

     The paper's group ratio is 1.83-1.86 across every seed, so the
     inequity is systematic. Ours is 0.954-0.974 across every seed.

3) The paper's own protocol (peak 2h/day, 1-hour steps, rate 0.05):
     max driver hours 14.0/week -> 0 of 200 drivers can reach the
     40h full-time threshold; service rate 2.6-3.0%; 99-106 of 200
     drivers idle; utilisation pinned at 0.99.
     The paper's setup structurally CANNOT measure either gap.
     This is why the main experiments use a full-day timeline.

====================================================================================================
LIMITATIONS REPORTED HONESTLY
====================================================================================================
1) The paper's prediction module gives no measurable benefit here.
   Removing it: utility 10,145 -> 10,290 (slightly HIGHER), rate_var 0.446 -> 0.406.
   We cannot reproduce the paper's Table 2 claim of a 41% utility
   collapse without prediction (95,824 -> 56,873).
2) The time-aware utility (Gap 1a) improves total utility by ~1% but does
   not by itself improve fairness. Its contribution is measurement
   correctness: 10.2% of utility signs flip. Reported as such.
3) Var(total) is worse for our method by construction, not by error.
4) Service rate is ~2 points below the paper method: a real, small cost.

====================================================================================================
ONE-LINE CONCLUSION
====================================================================================================
The paper equalises TOTAL earnings, which is the wrong target: it hides a
7.8x hourly-rate gap, actively widens the full-time/part-time gap to
1.62x, and never checks whether an available driver got work.
Re-targeting the objective at HOURLY RATE (with a within/between group
split) and UTILISATION brings the group ratio to 0.97x, cuts rate
inequity -64.4%, eliminates idle drivers, and costs -0.7% of total utility.
