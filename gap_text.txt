 Gap 1: Static Utility + Group-Blind Fairness 
 The paper's utility formula only uses distance: Utility =  Geo(dropoff,pickup) − 
 Geo(pickup,driver_location)  . It explicitly states  this stays the  same regardless of time  — so a 5km 
 trip at 2 PM and a 5km trip at peak 6 PM traffic are valued identically. Real traffic/time cost is 
 ignored. 
 Also, their fairness metric Var(driver earnings) treats all drivers equally regardless of hours 
 worked. 
 Example: If Driver A drives 60 hrs/week and earns ₹ 6000, and Driver B (part-timer) drives only 
 15 hrs/week and also earns ₹ 6000 — the paper calls this "perfectly fair" (variance = 0). But 
 Driver A's real rate is ₹ 100/hr while Driver B's is ₹ 400/hr. That's a hidden 4x gap the paper's 
 metric can't see. 
 Proposed fix: Make utility time/traffic-aware (reuse their existing demand-prediction module for 
 this), and split drivers into activity groups (full-time/part-time), then compare fairness within and 
 between these groups  using hourly rate  , not raw totals. 
 Gap 2: Utilization/Access Fairness 
 The paper only checks final earnings, never checks if an available driver was actually given 
 work. 
 Example: Driver X is online 8 hrs but assigned only 2 trips (idle 75% of time). Driver Y is online 
 8 hrs and assigned 10 trips. If X's 2 trips happen to be high-value, both can end up with equal 
 total earnings — paper says "fair." But X wasted 6 hours waiting with no assignment — that's a 
 real inequality the paper never measures. 
 Proposed fix: New metric — Utilization = assigned time-slots / online time-slots per driver, and 
 check its variance across drivers/groups.
